Week 1 of the tank: I bought and assembled all the necessary equipment (the 50cm sump-filtered tank, protein skimmer, wavemaker, return pump, salt, light, live rock filter media, roller filter, and some other small necessary parts). After that I added water and mixed in the salt, and used aquascaping putty and live rock to build a simple layout I liked. The putty's fast-drying, easily-cracking-early-on nature gave me a lot of trouble, and once it set (around 2 hours) and I added it to the tank, it released dust that made the water very cloudy (visible in the photos). It cleared up quickly, though. After that I added the sand bed. There were some small surprises on the live rock — a piece of sponge and something that looks like coral. I waited 2 days and then added the already-acclimated clownfish.

## [2026.8.31 - 2026.9.6] (Week 2)

Weekly maintenance tasks:

* Salinity: 1.024✅
* NH₃: ~0.15✅

Week 2 of the tank: I placed two small clownfish from another old tank into a breeding box inside the new saltwater tank, because I was worried they might fight due to the size difference. Last week, I noticed a heavy bloom of brown algae (diatoms), so I reduced both lighting and feeding. By the second week, I could clearly see the brown algae receding in the tank.
During observation, I noticed some bean‑sprout‑like plants growing on the live rock, as well as beautiful feather duster worms. At night, many amphipods (scuds) are active; they hide in rock crevices during the day and come out to forage around midnight.
The issue is that the brown algae haven't completely disappeared, and there are some Aiptasia (glass anemones) in the corners that need to be dealt with.

Week 2 of the tank: This week I noticed that the brown algae on the glass and live rock has receded a little, with some traces of green algae starting to show. It gets spooked easily in the tank, so it loves hiding in every nook and cranny of the rocks, and at night it even lies on the sand bed. But it’s gradually becoming more active.
